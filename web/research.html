Section 1

Genre & Market Precedents Beyond Suika Game

1. Inverted Gravity & Upward Buoyancy Physics

Standard drop-merge games (Suika Game, Drop7, Fruit Ninja Merge) rely almost exclusively on downward gravity. Upward vertical accumulation is rarely implemented:

  • Aqua Rush (Namco, 1999 — Arcade / System 12): Underwater block puzzle where multi-column bubble clusters rise upward from the seabed toward the surface. Players align columns to clear horizontal lines ("Rushes"). The definitive historical arcade precedent.
  • Nelly Jellies (MightyBig, 2023/2024 — itch.io / Mobile): Billed as a "reverse-gravity jelly merge puzzle." Players launch squishy jellies upward from the bottom of an aquarium. Jellies float upward and merge upon contact, hanging down from the surface ceiling.
  • Puzzle Uo Poko (Cave / Jaleco, 1998 — Arcade): Submersible firing buoyant bubbles upward with an analog plunger. Popped clusters free buoyant bubbles to rise to the ceiling.

2. Square/Box n×n Merge Mechanics with Physical Displacement

In circular merge games, objects easily roll off one another. Using rigid squares fundamentally alters the dynamics:

Frictional Locking & Arching Planar contact edges and 90° vertices form bridges and vertical columns that do not naturally settle into dips without lateral nudges.
Planar Hydraulic Wedge Push-Out When two n×n squares merge into an (n+1)×(n+1) square, the expanded bounding box exerts direct lateral normal forces on adjacent stacks, violently shoving neighbors aside.
  • Chain Cube 2048 3D (Mobile): Cubes launch and merge with physics impulse, knocking adjacent cubes outward and sideways.
  • Lumines (Q Entertainment, 2004) & Chime (2010): Constructing contiguous monochrome 2×2 square blocks that expand across the matrix.

3. Temperature / Pressure Resource Gauges & Defusal Win-Condition

Standard merge games are passive survival challenges. Introducing an active gauge creates dual-axis tension:

  • Magma Reactra (2025/2026): Molten lava orbs where merging spreads heat, requiring players to manage real-time chamber pressure before catastrophic detonations occur.
  • Wetrix (Zed Two, 1998 — N64/PC): Fireballs boil water away to reduce overflow risk; Ice blocks freeze water into un-drainable hazards.
  • Mr. Driller (Namco, 1999): Defusal pressure driven by a depleting air gauge (100% → 0%), providing a frantic race against the clock over falling-block physics.

4. Timing-Based Squeeze / Dimensional Deformation

  • sobosuba (keepeetron, August 2026): Drop-merge game with ultra-squishy soft-body blobs that compress and deform under pressure to squeeze through tight gaps.
  • Jelly Shift (SayGames): Dynamic aspect-ratio shifting: swiping up makes blocks tall and narrow to slip through vertical slits.
