Geometric Sketch: A Cube Divided into Pyramids

Clean architectural diagrams showing the two exact mathematical ways a cube decomposes into pyramids.

Method 1: Three Identical Pyramids Meeting at a Corner
Euclid's classic theorem: A cube can be cut along its internal diagonal planes into exactly 3 identical, congruent square pyramids.
How the math works:
1. Consider a cube with edge length $s$. Its volume is $s^3$.
2. Pick any corner $O = (0, 0, 0)$ as the apex.
3. Draw pyramids from $O$ to the three opposite faces ($x=s$, $y=s$, $z=s$).
4. Each pyramid has a square base of area $s^2$ and height $s$. By the volume formula of a pyramid ($\frac{1}{3} \times \text{Base} \times \text{Height}$), each pyramid has volume $\mathbf{\frac{1}{3} s^3}$.
5. Together, the three pyramids partition the entire cube with zero gaps and zero overlap!
Method 2: Six Pyramids Meeting at the Cube's Dead Center
Connecting the cube's 3D center point to each of its 6 square faces partitions the cube into 6 identical pyramids.
